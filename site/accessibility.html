Accessibility

This status page is for everyone, including during an outage when people may be stressed, on a phone, or using assistive technology. It is designed to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 Level AA. That goes beyond the WCAG 2.0 Level AA required by the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act (AODA) Integrated Accessibility Standards.

What we've done

  • Every status uses an icon shape and a text label, not just colour.
  • Text and controls meet WCAG contrast ratios in both light and dark mode. Centennial Green is used only as a background behind dark text, never as text on white.
  • Everything works with a keyboard, with a clearly visible focus outline and a "Skip to main content" link.
  • The page doesn't refresh on its own. When new information arrives you get an announcement and a "Show latest updates" button, so screen readers don't lose your place.
  • Pages reflow to 320 pixels wide and support text zoom to 200%, Windows High Contrast (forced colours) and reduced-motion settings.
  • Buttons and links are at least 44 by 44 pixels where possible, which makes them easier to tap.
  • Planned-work notices can be added to your calendar, so you don't need to remember to check back.
  • We write in plain language and tell you what to do, not just what's broken.

Known limitations of this prototype

  • This prototype needs JavaScript. The production version will be server-rendered and work without it.
  • The 90-day history bars give a summary to screen readers, but not day-by-day detail. The incident history page lists the same information as text.
  • The prototype has not yet had a formal audit or testing with assistive-technology users. That should happen before launch.
